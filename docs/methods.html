Methods and limitations

Dataset

The atlas serves 35 genome-scale metabolic models (GEMs) of 4 bacterial species. Each model carries reactions with bounds and gene-protein-reaction rules, metabolites with formula and charge, gene nucleotide sequences, growth rate anchors per medium axis, and a biomass reaction.

Union map

The 3D map shows the union of all 35 models: 3,706 compartment-specific metabolites (of which 197 are currency carriers such as ATP, NAD(H), water and protons) and 5,055 reactions, partitioned into 14 pathway groups. Metabolites sit at fixed positions on three concentric shells by compartment: extracellular (radius 42), periplasm (26) and cytosol (13). Metabolites of other compartments keep their assigned positions. An edge runs from each substrate to each product of a reaction; edges touching a currency metabolite render dimmed. Node and edge colour encode the pathway group.

Pathway search by enzyme presence (Discover)

The search enumerates simple paths from the substrate to the product over a directed metabolite graph. Each reaction contributes an edge from every substrate to every product; a reaction with a negative lower bound in at least one GEM also contributes the reverse edge. Currency metabolites are never used as intermediate nodes, so a pathway cannot pass through ATP, NAD(H), water or similar carriers; a currency metabolite may still be the chosen substrate or product. Compartment forms of a metabolite (_e, _p, _c) are distinct nodes joined by transport reactions.

Pathways are ranked by length, then by carrier count. Every result states whether the enumeration completed or stopped at a cap; a stopped search can miss pathways, and an empty result from a stopped search is not evidence of absence.

Strain presence means the strain's GEM contains at least one reaction for every step of the pathway. Presence does not check that the strain's own bounds permit each step in the required direction, does not check cofactor or redox balance, carbon conservation, thermodynamic feasibility, or flux feasibility; those checks belong to the Simulate stage. A pathway list is almost never complete: enumeration is bounded by the caps above and by the union network itself, which contains only reactions present in at least one of the 35 models.

Metabolite search fields

The substrate and product pickers match the metabolite id, the standard name and the KEGG compound id, case-insensitively. Of the 3,706 union-map metabolites, 2,279 carry a standard name and 1,704 a KEGG id; the remainder match by id only. Exact matches rank first, then prefix matches, then substring matches.

Flux feasibility (Simulate)

The Simulate stage evaluates found pathways in one chosen GEM on one medium by linear programming (GLPK 5.0 compiled to WebAssembly; every solve runs in the browser). The LP is steady-state mass balance S·v = 0 with the model's reaction bounds. Exchange reactions take their lower bound from the medium; an exchange not listed in the medium is closed (lb = 0). Secretion bounds keep the model's values. Bound edits made in the Model-stage reactions table apply to all feasibility solves in the same session.

A pathway is feasible on the medium when an LP solution exists that maximises a demand on the product while every pathway step carries at least 10-4 mmol gDW-1 h-1 of net flux in the pathway direction, summed over the step's alternative reactions present in the GEM. Infeasible means no such solution exists under the current bounds; it is a property of the GEM and medium, not of the organism. A pathway with a step whose reactions are all absent from the chosen GEM is not testable and is labelled so. The product target is the exchange reaction of the extracellular form when the GEM has one, otherwise an unbounded demand on the metabolite itself.

The reported flux distribution is parsimonious FBA: the product flux is fixed at 99.9% of its optimum and total absolute flux is minimised. FVA ranges are the minimum and maximum flux of each pathway reaction with the product held at 99% or more of its optimum. The growth rate reported for the run is a separate LP maximising the biomass reaction on the same medium, in h-1. Feasibility is tested automatically for the 10 shortest pathways of a search; the remaining pathways are tested one at a time from their result cards. Flux values are model predictions, not measurements.

Model stage fields

Mass balanced counts reactions whose elemental and charge balance closes with the recorded formulas; balanceable additionally counts reactions that close after proton adjustment. The gap-filled / orphan count is the model's reactions carrying no gene rule, excluding exchange, biomass and demand reactions; these entered the model without gene evidence and their knockouts cannot be implemented as gene deletions. GAM and NGAM are growth-associated and non-growth-associated ATP maintenance, in mmol ATP gDW-1 and mmol ATP gDW-1 h-1; the NGAM source is recorded per model. Growth rate anchors give the per-axis interval recorded in the model release, in h-1; they are model-derived values, not experimental measurements. Bound edits made in the reactions table are held in browser memory for the session; they are written to exports and apply to every feasibility solve. Reloading the page discards them.

Media

Nine predefined media ship with the data release. Each medium lists exchange reactions with uptake lower bounds, a carbon-source exchange and its uptake cap. A medium is editable in the Simulate panel: components can be added, removed or re-bounded, and a medium can be cloned to a named custom medium. Edited and custom media are held in browser memory for the session; reloading the page discards them. When a feasibility run starts, the chosen substrate replaces the medium's carbon source: the carbon-source exchange closes and the substrate's exchange opens at the medium's carbon cap. The swap can be switched off per run. A GEM with no exchange reaction for the substrate runs the medium unchanged, and the run says so. Medium components with no matching exchange in the chosen GEM are ignored when solving and counted in the editor.

Constraint-based analysis

The Analysis view runs on one GEM and one medium, with the same LP (S·v = 0, medium lower bounds on exchanges, session bound edits applied) as the Simulate stage. A knockout sets a reaction's bounds to [0, 0]; knockouts applied to the session are ordinary bound edits and feed every other solve, including the Simulate stage. All solving is GLPK 5.0 simplex without presolve: with the presolver on, this build of GLPK returned a solution violating a closed exchange bound by 1.25×10-4 on this dataset, so the presolver is off and any solution violating a column bound by more than 10-6 is refused and reported as unsolved. Values within 10-9 of zero print as zero.

Product target

The product is measured as its export: the exchange reaction of the extracellular form when the GEM has one (whichever compartment form was picked), otherwise an added demand, which is then the metabolite's only sink, so its minimum equals the guaranteed net production. When the medium leaves the product's exchange open for uptake, a negative floor means net uptake and the page says so.

Knockout sweep

The sweep closes each non-exchange, non-biomass reaction in turn and re-solves max growth; with a product target set and the knockout not lethal, it also solves the knockout's max product export and the product floor at biomass at or above the chosen fraction of the knockout's own max growth. A reaction whose session bounds are already [0, 0] is reported from the reference solve without a new LP and marked. Classes: lethal (max growth at or below 10-6 h-1, or no steady state), raises product floor (floor above the reference floor by more than 10-6), costly (max growth below 95% of reference), otherwise neutral. A knockout can be both costly and floor-raising; the floor label wins and both numbers stay in the table. A cancelled sweep keeps and exports the rows it computed, labelled as a partial sweep.

Shadow prices and reduced costs

The GLPK build used here does not expose LP dual values. Shadow prices are estimated at the max-growth optimum by finite differences: the metabolite's mass-balance right-hand side is moved from 0 to +10-3 and the LP re-solved; the estimate is the objective difference divided by the step, the marginal growth per unit of forced net production. If the positive step is infeasible the negative step is tried and the result labelled a left derivative; if both are infeasible (a conserved pool or blocked metabolite) no finite estimate exists and the entry says so. Reduced costs derive from these estimates as cj − Σ yi Sij over the reaction's own metabolites. On a reference model the estimates matched exact HiGHS duals to eight digits on non-degenerate rows; at a degenerate optimum a one-sided derivative can differ from the solver's dual, and estimates within 10-6 of zero should be read as zero.

Growth-coupling knockout search

Growth-coupled means the minimum product export is positive when biomass is held at or above a chosen fraction (default 90%) of the strain's own max growth. Equivalently, max growth with product export capped at 10-6 (decoupled growth) must fall below the fraction times max growth. The search is greedy: candidates are the reactions carrying flux above 10-6 in a parsimonious decoupled optimum (by default only reactions with a gene rule); each candidate is scored by the decoupled-to-max growth ratio its knockout produces, subject to a viability floor on max growth (default 10% of the reference); the best is applied and the loop repeats up to K knockouts (default 3). The search stops with a coupled verdict, or reports exactly why it stopped: no growth, product not producible (a knockout never adds capability), no viable candidate, no improvement, or K exhausted. A not-coupled verdict is a property of this greedy search, not a proof that no coupling knockout set exists. Yields are flux ratios at the floor solution (mmol product per mmol substrate uptake; C-mol basis from the recorded formulas when both carry a carbon count) and are model predictions.

Production envelope

The envelope fixes biomass at each of N levels (default 24) from 0 to max growth and solves max and min product export at each: 2N + 1 LPs. Points whose LP fails are reported and not drawn. With a coupled knockout set from the search, a second envelope is solved with those knockouts applied and overlaid.

FSEOF expression targets

The FSEOF scan (flux scanning with enforced objective flux) enforces the product's export at 11 evenly spaced levels (10 by default above zero, adjustable 4 to 20) from 0 to 95% of its maximum; the top level stops below 100% because at the exact maximum the optimum is a single point where the growth LP is often degenerate. At each level biomass is maximised and the distribution is made parsimonious (pFBA at 99.9% or more of that level's own max growth); a level whose pFBA fails reports the plain optimum and is labelled. A reaction is an amplification (over-expression) target when its |flux| rises monotonically across the solved levels (tolerance 10-6 per step) by more than 10-4 mmol gDW-1 h-1 in total, and an attenuation (down-regulation) target when it falls monotonically by the same margin. Reactions that change flux direction across the scan, or never carry flux, are in neither list. Exchanges, the biomass reaction and the product target are out of scope. The slope is the least-squares slope of |flux| against enforced product flux. Because growth falls as the enforced product rises, the attenuation list includes reactions that shrink only because biomass synthesis shrinks; the amplification list is the specific one. Each level's flux distribution is one parsimonious optimum among possibly several, so membership near the thresholds can depend on the solver's choice of optimum.

Linear MOMA

The MOMA prediction (minimisation of metabolic adjustment) is the linear (L1) variant: it minimises the summed absolute deviation |v - vwt| over all reactions, subject to S·v = 0 and the bounds with the chosen reactions closed. Each deviation is split into a positive and a negative part, which makes the problem an LP; the original quadratic (L2) MOMA objective needs a quadratic solver and is not computed here. The wild-type reference vwt is the parsimonious distribution at 99.9% or more of max growth under the session's current bounds, which is one of possibly several optimal distributions; the MOMA prediction is relative to that choice. The FBA comparison columns re-solve the same knockout under growth maximisation, with the product read from a parsimonious solution. An infeasible MOMA LP means no steady-state flux distribution satisfies the knockout bounds (including any maintenance requirement), and is reported as such.

Flux sampling

Sampling is random-objective vertex sampling: each sample is the optimal solution of one random dense linear objective, with coefficients drawn standard-normal for every reaction from a seeded generator, over the feasible flux space of the GEM, medium and session bounds; biomass can optionally be held at or above a chosen fraction of max growth. Every sample is a vertex of the solution polytope. The empirical distributions therefore describe the polytope's boundary as seen from random directions and are an approximation; they are not a uniform sample of the flux space, and interior points are never produced. A uniform sampler (such as ACHR or CHRR) is not implemented. Objectives whose LP fails the optimality or bound check contribute no sample and are counted. Per-reaction summaries report mean, median, the 5-95% interquantile range, and the minimum and maximum over the solved samples; the same seed with the same inputs reproduces the same samples.

Model export

The Model stage exports the loaded model in two formats, both built in the browser at export time with the session's bound edits written in and counted in the file notes. A medium is a solve-time constraint and is not written into exported bounds; exports carry the model's own (possibly edited) bounds.

The SBML export is level 3 version 1 with the fbc version 2 package (fbc:strict): species with compartment, charge and chemical formula; reactions with stoichiometry and flux bounds as shared constant parameters in mmol gDW-1 h-1; gene associations as fbc gene-product associations parsed from the model's gene rules; and the biomass reaction as the active maximisation objective. A formula that is not strictly elemental (averaged lipid compositions, dotted adducts) is omitted from the SBML rather than written as an invalid value; the JSON export keeps the raw string. A gene rule that fails to parse is omitted from the SBML and counted in the notes. The JSON export follows the COBRA schema (version 1) with the model's identifiers unchanged and cross-references as identifier-style annotations. Both formats round-trip through libSBML and COBRApy; on the reference model the re-imported SBML reproduces this site's growth LP optimum to within 10-10.

Release scope

This release covers Discover pathway search, Simulate flux feasibility (pFBA and FVA on an editable medium), the 3D union map, the Model stage with its dashboard and SBML and COBRA JSON export, and the Analysis view: single-reaction knockout sweeps, shadow-price and reduced-cost estimates, greedy growth-coupling search, production envelopes, FSEOF expression targets, linear MOMA knockout predictions and flux sampling. Not implemented: quadratic (L2) MOMA, uniform flux samplers (ACHR/CHRR), double-knockout sweeps, and thermodynamic or kinetic constraints.
